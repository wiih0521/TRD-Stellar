For $d=\gcd(a,b)$, $ax+by=c$ is solvable iff $d\mid c$. Given one solution
$(x_0,y_0)$, all solutions are
\[
 x=x_0+k\frac bd,\qquad y=y_0-k\frac ad,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]
\begin{align*}
 \varphi(n)&=n\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac1p\right),&
 \sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)&=n,\\
 a^{\varphi(n)}&\equiv1\pmod n &&(\gcd(a,n)=1).
\end{align*}
\[
 \mu(n)=\begin{cases}
 0&\text{$n$ is not square-free},\\
 (-1)^k&\text{$n$ is a product of $k$ distinct primes},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 g(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}f(d)
\]
\[
 \Longleftrightarrow\qquad
 f(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)g(n/d).
\]
Primitive roots exist modulo $2,4,p^k,2p^k$ for odd prime $p$.
For prime $p$, $a^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p$ when $p\nmid a$.
